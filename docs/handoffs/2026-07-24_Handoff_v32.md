## Completed

Three design calls decided and shipped:

- **#327 -> PR #351: toasts queue one at a time.** The blind run reported a
  stale message sitting for minutes and three toasts burying the actionable line
  on arrival. An auto-fade would have fixed both but reverses the
  hold-until-Space decision tuned across three sessions. Owner's call was no
  timer, each toast needing its own press. Implemented as a queue: one message
  on screen, the rest wait behind it, each costs a press, and the dismiss cue
  carries the waiting count so the queue is discoverable. A repeat of a queued
  message is dropped, since several toasts fire on a condition rather than an
  event. Rules are pure in `src/systems/toast-queue.ts`.
- **#300 -> PR #352: blocking overlays pause the world.** Only dialogue froze
  the wagon; the journal, skills, codex, and upgrade menu were cosmetic layers
  over live gameplay. The persona playtest had measured what that allowed: 12
  tiles driven with wear, a delivery paid out under the panel, R repairing, T
  crossing a region gateway, and a road encounter opening its dialogue on top of
  the open journal. Owner's call was full modal. Only panel input now runs while
  a panel is up.
- **#299 -> PR #353: reconnection notes for the three pocket settlements.**
  Reedgrave, Saltmere, and Fenholt are the premium pocket destinations and the
  hardest deliveries in the game, and all three fell back to the generic
  reconnection line. Three notes authored, each answering the specific cargo,
  none naming what is inside a sealed cargo (the deliberately-left-open
  guardrail). Text approved by the owner before landing, per the lore rule.

## Decisions taken

Recorded here because the issues that carried them are now closed:

1. **Toasts: queue, do not fade.** The tuned hold survives. Rationale and the
   rejected alternative are in `docs/design/05_playtest_notes.md`.
2. **Overlays: modal.** Nobody had ever asked to act behind a panel, one player
   was surprised to find they had been driving behind one, and the
   encounter-over-journal case broke the one-thing-at-a-time rule the D1 pass
   committed to. Recorded as item 5 in `docs/design/08_ui_and_onboarding.md`.
3. **Pocket notes: author all three, and enforce it.** A region invariant now
   fails if any contract destination lacks bespoke text. No contract delivers to
   a home settlement, so the strict rule needs no exemption list, which is what
   makes it maintainable. Recorded in `docs/design/04_storyline.md`.
4. **#229 (visual region editor) closed as not planned** on scope, not on the
   JSON premise, which its body had already corrected. See v31.

## Risks or decisions needed

- **None of today's three UX changes has been played by a human.** They were
  verified by unit tests, browser specs, neutralize checks, and screenshots,
  which is the strongest automated evidence available and still cannot answer
  "does it feel right". Two of them change moment-to-moment feel directly: the
  toast queue adds a press per message, and modal overlays remove the
  peek-while-driving a habituated player may be used to. A short playtest is the
  real signal, and it is the same unblock the tuning thread has been waiting on.
- The toast queue's press-per-message cost scales with how many messages fire at
  once. Arrival at a settlement can raise three. If that reads as tedious in
  play, the lever is fewer toasts (route some to the event log), not a timer,
  which the decision above deliberately rejected.
