## Decisions taken

1. **The press-count complaint was measured, not guessed.** A scripted driver
   played four Greybridge loops counting every non-movement press: 41 presses, 29
   of them Space. That measurement is what split one vague complaint into a clear
   defect (#376) and a design call (#378). After both, the same four loops cost 29
   presses; a delivery arrival went 5 presses to 2 and the steady-state loop 7 to 4.
2. **#378 was filed as a design call rather than built**, because reducing the
   arrival stack meant changing the #327 one-press-per-message rule, which the owner
   had already iterated on twice, and `tests/e2e/toast-queue.spec.ts` existed
   specifically to pin it. Four options went to the owner, who picked grouping.
3. **Grouping is by burst, not by "is there room".** A message raised seconds later
   must not appear inside a panel the player is part-way through reading, which is
   the #327 guarantee that actually mattered. The game frame is the burst stamp, so
   "raised together" is exactly "raised in one update()", and `showToast`'s ~25 call
   sites did not change.
4. **Audio ships synthesized before sampled**, following the project's own phased
   art strategy. No binaries, no licence entries, no bundle weight, and it still
   exercises everything that can be got wrong. Committing binaries before the wiring
   was proven would have been the wrong order.
5. **The audio mix has teeth**, mirroring `juice.ts`: stranding is the loudest cue
   and accepting a contract the quietest, with failures falling in pitch and good
   moments rising. A unit test pins the ordering so a retune cannot quietly turn it
   into a rewards-lead mix.
6. **The mute hint is one-directional.** `V: sound` shows only while muted. That
   line is context-sensitive because printing every key read as noise, and a player
   who has sound does not need telling they can mute; a player who muted and forgot
   does, because silence is indistinguishable from broken audio.
7. **Driving was scoped silent, then overturned the same day.** The original
   reasoning (continuous, and the wear meter carries it) treated audio purely as
   punctuation on discrete events. A rolling bed whose timbre follows terrain makes
   "roads are gameplay" audible in a way no meter does. Recorded in the design note
   rather than quietly replaced, because the note argued the opposite.

## Verification notes

- **Ten guards were neutralized and watched to fail before being restored** (trap
  1), across four PRs. The two most valuable: deleting the `audio.delivered()` call
  site made the e2e report `Received: null`, which is the exact
  function-with-no-caller shape the audio e2e hook exists to catch; and disabling
  toast grouping failed with "expected > 1, received 1".
- **Every PR ran the full browser suite**, not just the arc.
- **Audio needed a verification method that did not exist yet.** `synthesizeCue` is
  wrapped in a catch so a cue never breaks a frame, which means a broken schedule
  would be *silently* silent with every test still green: trap 1's second shape. So
  the real function was imported through the Vite dev server into an
  `OfflineAudioContext`, rendered, and the samples measured. All eight throw nothing
  in a real implementation and peak within 6% of target.
- **A measurement harness was wrong before the code was.** Twice. The first
  press-count run reported 12-press blocks that turned out to be the driver pressing
  Space into a modal dialogue where `handleDismissInput` never runs. The first cue
  renderer flagged all eight cues as broken because it asserted the audible span
  within 25ms of the nominal duration, when an exponential decay drops below the
  audibility floor at about 72% of it. Both times the harness was fixed, not the
  game. Worth remembering: trap 1 says a green suite proves nothing, and the
  converse also holds.
- **Two AudioContexts exist per document.** Phaser's own sound manager creates one
  at boot (suspended, unused by Phase 1) and `audio.ts` creates the one it plays
  through. Harmless at two, worth knowing before Phase 2 uses Phaser's loader.

## Risks or decisions needed

- **The audio mix has never been heard by a human at volume.** The cue numbers were
  reasoned and then measured, and the owner confirmed they "work and make sense",
  but nobody has judged whether the stranded cue lands as the moment that hurt or
  merely as an irritation, or whether the delivery cue wears thin after twenty of
  them. Retuning now is eight rows of numbers in `src/systems/audio-cues.ts`; after
  Phase 2 swaps in samples it is an asset job.
- **The bed is the first continuous voice in the game.** Every cue so far is a
  one-shot, and the existing "last requested cue" e2e hook cannot describe a
  continuous sound. #383 needs its own observable state or the bed is unprovable,
  which is trap 1 again.
- **`map-scene.ts` grew and nothing tracks it.** Same paragraph as v35, one session
  later, and now with audio call sites added.
- **Release drift starts over.** 0.7.0 shipped today and four PRs have landed since
  the tag, so the next release window is already open.
- **Phase 2 audio will add the first binary assets and the first new licence
  entries since the sprite packs.** Both `assets/credits.md` and the hand-maintained
  `credits.html` need entries, and the two drift silently because nothing checks
  them against each other.
