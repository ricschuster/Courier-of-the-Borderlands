# Session Handoff: 2026-07-25 (v33)

## Summary

Continues from v32, which closed out the 2026-07-24 design calls. This stretch
ran a seven-player persona playtest against those same-day changes, then fixed
both things it found. The playtest caught a regression introduced earlier in the
session, which is the outcome it existed to produce.

The tracker is down to a single open issue.

## Completed

- **Persona playtest (PR #357).** Seven players against the day's three merges:
  four scripted arc personas (completionist, miser, wayfinder, panel-hopper),
  four instrumented exploit probes, and a blind screenshot-only session. All
  scripted personas reached the capstone. No crashes, soft locks, or runtime
  errors. Report: `docs/playtests/2026-07-24_persona_playtest.md`.
- **#355 -> PR #358: each modal surface states its own controls.** Both modal
  branches of `update()` return before `refreshHint()`, so the hint line froze
  when a panel or conversation opened and went on advertising keys the #352
  freeze had made inert ("WASD/arrows drive" with driving stopped). Each surface
  now lists only keys that work, and the world hint returns on the frame the
  surface closes.
- **#356 -> PR #359: panel refusals render in the panel.** Seven refused upgrade
  presses cost seven dismiss presses under the #327 queue; they now cost zero.
  Successes stay toasts, and refusals with no panel on screen stay toasts.

## What the playtest established

- **The #352 modal freeze is airtight and free.** The panel-hopper opened and
  closed an overlay roughly 100 times across a full arc, checking each time that
  neither wear nor position moved. Zero leaks, and its economy came out
  identical to the completionist's (710 vs 709 coins, 14.0 vs 13.8 wear per
  delivery).
- **The #327 queue is cheaper than feared.** ~2.4 dismiss presses per delivery,
  max depth 6 after an arrival. v32 flagged this as a risk; it is real but small.
- **The economy still has teeth and reproduces the 2026-07-16 controls.**
  Completionist repair tax 59% (was 56%), miser 100% (was 100%), miser wall time
  2.2x the completionist (same 2.2x as last time). The miser finishes with zero
  voluntary spending but pays 7 strandings and ends on 3 coins against 709.
- **Wayfinder is still economically dominated.** All points into Wayfinder while
  buying every upgrade costs 43% more wear per delivery, strands once, and ends
  with 1 coin. #341's survey ring adds a visibility payoff the run cannot
  measure; the economics behind #324 are unchanged.
- **Every probed exploit stayed closed**: reload tow, gateway travel behind a
  panel, toast survival through a conversation, duplicate suppression.

## Decisions taken

1. **Playtest tooling: throwaway, not adopted.** Persona specs were written
   under `tests/e2e/`, run, and deleted; only the report and the issues persist.
   This respects #328, which retired the previous untracked harness and said to
   rebuild with an ADR if one is ever wanted as a real tool.
2. **Refusals belong in the panel, not the toast queue (#356).** Chosen over all
   four options originally listed on that issue, because checking the call sites
   showed every noisy refusal can only fire while its panel is open. `buyUpgrade`
   has a single caller, gated on the menu.

## Risks or decisions needed

- **The toast and freeze guards are coupled through one assertion.**
  `overlay-freeze.spec.ts` proved panel input still runs under the #352 freeze by
  watching a refusal toast appear. #356 deleted that observable, so the spec
  failed on the full run and now watches the panel notice instead. The suite
  caught it, which is the intended outcome, but anyone touching either area
  should know the two are linked there.
- **Still no human playtest.** v32 raised this and it stands. The persona run
  answers mechanical questions well and cannot answer whether anything feels
  right. Two changes since v32 alter moment-to-moment feel further.
- **The blind persona replay is not deterministic.** Weather is randomised per
  boot and changes how far a fixed key-hold drives, so a scripted blind session
  diverges between runs. Fine for finding things, not for regression.
- The playtest driver's own `deliveries` and `income` counters were wrong (the
  first read a per-region field, the second missed deltas). Both are excluded
  from the report's matrix and declared in it. A future harness needs the
  delivery-reward breakdown recorded directly, as #328 already noted.
